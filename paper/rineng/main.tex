\begin{abstract}
Museum HVAC systems run year-round at energy intensities two to three
times those of office buildings, because conservation envelopes require
narrow-band control of temperature and relative humidity. Existing
controllers either treat conservation limits as soft reward penalties,
conflicting with the safety semantics of EN~15757 and ASHRAE Class~A1,
or use classical multi-objective optimization that cannot adapt to
time-varying loads. We formulate museum HVAC control as a Constrained
Markov Decision Process that aggregates ASHRAE static envelopes and the
EN~15757 dynamic rate caps on $|\mathrm{d}T/\mathrm{d}t|$ and
$|\mathrm{d}\mathrm{RH}/\mathrm{d}t|$ into a scalar cost, and solve it
with a Lagrangian Proximal Policy Optimization controller whose
multiplier is updated by a PID loop. We also document an implementation
pitfall in subprocess-based vectorized training: the multiplier must be
pushed to workers via the \texttt{set\_attr} mechanism. On a five-zone
simulator, a six-budget Pareto sweep shows annual energy varying by only
$0.4\%$ while the year-total conservation cost varies by a factor of
$2.5$; at the strictest budget the controller cuts conservation cost by
$85\%$ and violation steps by $41\%$ versus a fixed $22\,^\circ$C$/50\%$RH
baseline, at a $1\%$ energy overhead, with residual violations confined
to a single constraint-coupled zone. We then stress-test these
conclusions on a high-fidelity EnergyPlus model extended with per-zone
thermostats and active humidification. The localization finding
transfers, but the performance advantage does not: there a tuned fixed
setpoint outperforms the constrained controller, because the energy
gradient overwhelms the constraint signal. We release the testbed.
\end{abstract}

\begin{highlights}
\item EN 15757 temperature and humidity rate caps encoded as a CMDP cost.
\item Lagrangian-PPO traces an energy--conservation Pareto front on a 5-zone simulator.
\item Residual violations localize to one constraint-coupled (bronze) zone.
\item A lambda-propagation pitfall in subprocess vectorized training is documented.
\item On high-fidelity EnergyPlus, RL is beaten by a tuned fixed setpoint.
\end{highlights}
